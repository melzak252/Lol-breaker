The audit distinguishes five requirements:

1. Useful sports forecasts on their declared sports cohort.
2. Incremental predictive information conditional on the market.
3. Reliable payout-weighted probabilities on the bets actually selected.
4. Positive after-cost policy value at genuinely available prices.
5. Valid execution and risk accounting.

None implies all the others. A competitive sports model can lose money without a side/BO/payout bug. Conversely, a profitable small backtest does not certify its EV estimates.

## 3. What the literature establishes

### 3.1 Calibration must name its information basis and decision population

Forecast-only calibration, approximately `E[Y|p]=p`, does not imply `E[Y|p,q,O,selected]=p`. Gneiting and Resin distinguish calibration relative to the forecast from being ideal relative to a richer information basis. Multicalibration extends guarantees only to declared, sufficiently supported groups. Decision-calibration guarantees concern average decisions/losses in the declared class, not the correctness of every individual bet. [1][2][3][4]

This is the central mismatch in the application: a sports forecast is selected precisely because it disagrees with a price. That selected population can expose errors invisible in an overall reliability diagram or average LL. Good ECE is not enough; proper scores and useful discrimination still matter. [1][6]

**Price conditioning under a fixed policy is not automatically the optimizer's curse.** The optimizer's curse concerns selecting alternatives using noisy estimated values. Choosing the model, EV threshold, league exclusions, horizon or staking rule after seeing historical results adds that separate selection problem. Both issues matter here, but they should not be conflated. [5][26][27]

Sports-betting papers sometimes recommend calibration over classification accuracy. The NBA experiment using closing odds in Walsh/Joshi addresses that comparison, not whether a model already evaluated by LL/Brier has trustworthy market-conditional EV in LoL. Dmochowski's theory addresses a specified sports-betting decision problem with NFL spread/total assumptions; it is not a validation of our binary series-winner implementation. Neither paper supplies a profitable LoL recipe. [7][8]

Changing the label/loss is not the first remedy. At a fixed quote, return-MSE is exactly `(0.88O)^2 * (p−y)^2`: an odds-squared-weighted Brier loss. If informative odds are omitted from the inputs, weighting can change the conditional estimand rather than recover the ordinary sports probability. This algebra and its limits were already recorded in the [September 27 EV-target study](ev_overestimation_targets_and_plan_20260927.md); no new target or return-loss experiment was run here. First test the small market-conditional probability model against proper market-only controls. [6]

### 3.2 Test sports information against a strong market-only model

Koning/Zijm explicitly test additional information after a flexible market-probability transformation. A raw-q comparator alone can confuse market recalibration with added sports information. Probability forecast-encompassing and conditional predictive-ability work likewise distinguishes incremental forecast content from unconditional accuracy. The latter two papers were accessed at abstract level only; no inaccessible theorem or implementation detail is relied upon. [9][11][12]

Use a market-only slope and a small odd nonlinear shape control, alongside the sports-plus-market candidate. A fitted sports coefficient is not by itself an adoption result. Assess out-of-time LL/Brier, coverage and the selected policy after the full recipe is frozen.

Markets are not perfect probabilities. Soccer research finds differences between market types and margin-removal assumptions; odds-derived ratings can beat traditional Elo without outperforming the odds themselves. Return-based evaluation and forecast accuracy are distinct questions. None establishes that a LoL sports-only model will profit against Polish books. [10][13][14]

### 3.3 Margin allocation, reference price and executable price are separate

For a two-way quote, let `r_A=1/O_A`, `r_B=1/O_B`:

- Proportional: `q_A=r_A/(r_A+r_B)`.
- Power: solve a positive exponent `k` satisfying `r_A^k+r_B^k=1`, then `q_A=r_A^k`.
- Additive: `q_A=r_A−(r_A+r_B−1)/2`; use only valid probabilities. In the binary case, Shin and additive coincide under the corresponding formulation.

These are alternative margin-allocation assumptions, not truth estimators certified for our sport. The CRAN vignette provides the formulas; the Clarke comparison's abstract supports comparing methods, not claiming a universal winner. Favorite–longshot bias and strategic price setting are documented in other populations, with no automatic transfer to LoL or every bookmaker. [15][16][17][18]

Construct a complete outcome vector **within each bookmaker**, then give each admitted book one vote. Separately select the best actually available side price. Combining the best A and best B prices into a supposedly representative probability vector changes the object being estimated.

For a bet executed at book b, a leave-b-out reference with at least two remaining books tests whether apparent value depends on the selected quote itself. It does not make peer books statistically independent or prove that their consensus is correct. Unsupported rows must remain absent, not be filled with later quotes.

### 3.4 Tax and execution set a demanding hurdle

For an ordinary cash-funded single under the replay's 12% stake-tax assumption:

`EV = 0.88 * O * p − 1`, so `p_break_even = 1/(0.88O)` and `p_5% = 1.05/(0.88O)`.

At odds **2.00**, break-even requires **56.82%**, and +5% net EV requires **59.66%**. A 50% fair chance at 2.00 loses 12% of stake in expectation. Relative to raw `1/O`, break-even requires a **13.64%** probability increase; +5% requires **19.32%**, before any additional margin-allocation issue.

The Polish Ministry of Finance confirms the 12% rate for the relevant category. STS documents the .88 coefficient and a possible additional 10% large-win deduction; accepted stake, limits and operator rules also matter. Its help prose says “equal to or above 2280,” while its quoted statutory exemption says “does not exceed 2280.” This report does **not** silently choose the exact boundary. [19][20][21]

A quote is not an accepted coupon. Promotional funding, stake acceptance, odds changes, voids, partial settlement, rounding and actual result availability require their own cash-flow evidence. A formula using .88 is not exhaustive execution certification.

### 3.5 Kelly is not a probability-repair procedure

Kelly maximizes expected log growth under the assumed conditional payoff law. Bayesian or robust variants introduce a model/prior/ambiguity set; those assumptions do not become a distribution-free lower bound on each C0 probability. Fractional Kelly and caps alter exposure, not whether the edge is valid. Simultaneous correlated exposures need more than independent per-match fractions. [22][23][24]

A useful distinction is:

`model-implied PnL on saved stakes = sum_i[p_i * round_cent(0.88*S_i*O_i) − S_i]`.

This sums one-step plug-in expectations along the realized simulated stake path. It is neither expected log wealth nor an ex-ante expectation of final bankroll. Conditioning literally on the entire future realized stake path can reveal previous outcomes; the phrase “conditional expectation” must not obscure that distinction.

### 3.6 Resampling and calibration labels do not create evidence

Five observed months remain five blocks after 5,000 resamples. Independence across declared clusters and adequate cluster support matter. Search over exposed backtests, reused holdouts and unknown effective trial counts is not cured by reporting an ordinary bootstrap interval or a deflated-Sharpe label. [25][26][27]

Preregistration must specify the choices that otherwise remain flexible. Confidence sequences permit certain forms of sequential inference under their conditional-mean/boundedness assumptions; they do not automatically certify a drifting, adaptively selected betting policy. Conformal risk control requires its own exchangeability and bounded monotone-loss conditions; thresholding EV is not automatically such a loss family. [28][29][30]

Under an explicitly hypothetical PLN interpretation, large-win thresholds use the **cent-rounded post-12%-stake-tax winning amount**, not `stake*odds` before that deduction. Across **2,956 saved ledger rows**, **8** potential winning payouts exceed 2,280, **0** actual winning rows do, and **0** equal the boundary. These are duplicated strategy/model/origin ledger rows, not eight independent bets. The first-observed C0 core has **zero** such potential payouts under either staking strategy. The possible additional 10% deduction therefore does not explain that portfolio's loss. It still belongs in any future accepted-coupon contract. [19][20][21]

No actual denomination, coupon tax settlement or promotional funding was authenticated. Source boundary wording is unresolved rather than guessed.

### 5.3 Roster repair matters, but is not a complete financial remedy

On the same **34 first-observed C0 selections**, the retained actual-lineup substitution changes **10** rosters and reduces mean predicted EV from **24.54% to 22.43%**.
